ASHA Companion

A triage app for the health worker already at the door: treat what’s safe, refer the rest with evidence.

Role
Fieldwork with classmates; the concept, flows and UI are mine
Course
Designing for social needs, UPES Dehradun
Timeline and methods
Nov – Dec 2025: street and site interviews, a clinic doctor, an ASHA questionnaire, two facility visits, desk research
Overview

In Dehradun’s bastis (informal settlements), a fever is often waited out, because every route to care costs a day’s wage or cash. ASHA Companion turns the ASHA, the government health worker who already visits these homes, into a first responder, backed by a doctor she doesn’t have to wait for.

The case study follows a house call: the history, the diagnosis, then the prescription.

Who people turn to first, in the order they described it. The ASHA lives in the second ring, but nobody named her as someone who treats.

Free care stops being free once you count the day it takes

For a daily-wage worker, the government centre is paid for in time, the basti clinic in cash, and waiting costs nothing until it does.

The doctor is the scarcest thing in the system

The norm is one doctor for every thousand people. In Uttarakhand, each government doctor serves almost eight times that, and the hills are emptier still.

Someone already visits every home, and she spends the visit on paperwork

India pays an ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist) from each community to visit its households. We asked 22 of them about their day, their apps and what they feel allowed to do.

The system already pays someone to visit every home. It never let her decide anything.

I can’t add doctors, so each doctor’s minute has to go further

eSanjeevani, the government’s teleconsult app, already exists. It asks the patient and the doctor to be online at the same moment, which the hills rarely allow.

The reasoning in one chain: doctors are scarce and won’t move to the hills. The ASHA is already in the home but won’t act without backing. So she triages. Safe cases end at her door, and the rest reach a doctor as a packet he reviews in his own time.

Every part of the prescription answers a symptom we heard

Five symptoms from the fieldwork, five parts of the design. Nothing in the app exists without one.

One house call, from knock to verdict

Step through the five screens as the ASHA would. Each one says which part of the prescription it carries.

Two channels: solved at the door, or sent with a file

Every case leaves with a tag, like triage in an emergency ward. Green ends at home. Red travels to a doctor who never had to be online with her.

The app’s most important job is saying no. A danger sign skips the kit and goes straight to the red channel.

Working thresholds would come from the state’s standing orders and a clinician, not from me.

The app is her rough notebook, and it writes the fair copy

Today she writes in the register during the visit, then types it all again into an app. Here, the taps become the register line.
