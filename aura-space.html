A wayfinding concept that tells transit travellers how long they have, so waiting at the gate becomes a choice.

Overview

In most of Changi, security screening happens at each gate, and the holdroom behind it has little to do. Aura Space is a speculative concept that gives transit travellers a live countdown and a route, so they can leave the gate area knowing when to come back.

The case study reads like a trip through the airport: check-in, security, boarding, then arrivals, where I check which of my assumptions landed.

Check-in: Discover

Every sign says where. None says how long.

How is Changi laid out for someone in transit, and what can’t they see from where they stand?

At most of Changi, security waits at your gate

Terminals 1 to 3 screen passengers at each gate. Go through early and the toilets, food and shops stay on the other side.

Travellers can read their gate, not their distance

The screens answer where to go. The question that decides when to leave the mall gets no answer in one place.

Security: Define

Past this line, the airport shrinks to one room.

What are travellers trading when they go through early, and what should a design never do to them?

The gate buys certainty, and the traveller pays with everything else

I called it the gate trap: when you can’t tell how long you have, going through early feels safe. It is my inference from desk research, not something I watched happen.

How might the gate become a place travellers choose to go, not one they hide in?

Five conditions of carriage, set before any screen

A traveller in transit has a pass, a phone and no patience. These rules decided what got built and what got cancelled.

Boarding: Design

Let the phone hold the countdown.

An old kiosk, a browser tab and a few chevrons in the air: no new hardware for Changi, and no app for the traveller.

From the kiosk to the gate in four moments

Each moment answers one question: how long do I have, which way, what’s near, and when must I go back.

Phone down, eyes up: one screen that does most of the work

Held low, the tab shows a single arrow and the countdown. The camera stays off until the traveller raises the phone.

AR in a crowded corridor needs rules a showroom never would

Most AR wayfinding demos run in empty halls. Changi’s transit mall has trolleys, families and overhead signs, so I set four placement rules.

One warning, in the colour the airport already uses

Gate screening is the one step a traveller can’t easily undo, so it is the one moment the design interrupts.
